Search a 2D Matrix

You can follow every step of the solution here. Pick the number to search for, press Next, and see which cell of the matrix is checked and which line of the code runs.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    The number-guessing game

    I'm thinking of a number between 1 and 100; after each of your guesses I say "higher" or "lower". The smartest guess is always in the middle, because every answer eliminates half of the remaining numbers. That is binary search.

  2. 2

    The matrix is really a sorted list

    Every row is sorted, and every row starts with a number larger than the last number of the previous row. If you put the rows end to end, you get a single sorted list: 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 16, 20, 23, 30, 34, 60. We can play the guessing game on this list.

  3. 3

    Read the list without copying it

    The number at position k of the list sits in the matrix at row = k / 4, column = k % 4 (4 = the number of columns). / drops the fraction and % gives the remainder. For example, 9 / 4 = 2 and 9 % 4 = 1, so number 9 is matrix[2][1] = 30.

Why is it so fast?

Every look halves the range

Every look cuts the remaining range in half: 12 → 6 → 3 → 1. In this 12-number example, at most 4 looks are enough.

Only 14 looks even in the largest matrix

Even in the largest matrix on LeetCode (100 × 100 = 10,000 cells), at most 14 looks are enough. Visiting every cell one by one would mean 10,000 looks.
