Happy Number

Step through whether the chain of digit-square sums reaches 1 or falls into an endless cycle, using Floyd's Tortoise and Hare pointers in O(1) space.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    The chain can't grow forever

    Even for the largest 32-bit integer, the sum of the digit squares is at most 810, so every number either reaches 1 or enters a cycle.

  2. 2

    Two pointers at two speeds

    On each step, slow takes the digit-square sum once and fast takes it twice. There is no need to keep an extra HashSet.

  3. 3

    Stop at 1 or meet in the cycle

    If fast == 1, the number is happy. If there is a cycle, the fast pointer catches up with the slow one (slow == fast) and false is returned.

Why is it so efficient?

Why can't the numbers grow forever?

Even for 9999, the largest 4-digit number, the sum of the digit squares is 4 × 81 = 324. For any 3-digit number, the sum can't exceed 243. So within a few steps, every number is trapped in the range [1, 243].

Floyd's cycle-detection idea

If two runners are on a circular track and one runs twice as fast as the other, the faster one always catches up with the slower one from behind. The gap between them closes by 1 on each step, and within as many steps as the cycle length they meet on the same number.

Two pointers instead of a HashSet

Storing the numbers you've seen in a HashSet also works, but it allocates extra memory and computes hashes. With two integer variables (slow and fast), memory usage is O(1).

The 8-element cycle of unhappy numbers

Every positive integer that can't reach 1 eventually enters the same 8-element cycle: 4 → 16 → 37 → 58 → 89 → 145 → 42 → 20 → 4.
