Hand of Straights

Once we sort the cards and count their frequencies, the smallest remaining card must always be the start of a new consecutive group. Watch the groups form step by step.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    Check divisibility and sort

    If the total number of cards isn't divisible by groupSize, return false right away. Otherwise, sort the cards in ascending order and record how many of each card there are in the count dictionary.

  2. 2

    Start from the smallest remaining card

    While walking the sorted array, skip cards whose count is 0; the first card with a count > 0 must be the head of a new group.

  3. 3

    Decrement groupSize consecutive cards

    Take one of each of the cards card, card+1, …, card+groupSize-1. If any of them is missing, the group can't be formed (false).

Why is it so efficient?

Why is the greedy choice always correct?

When the smallest remaining card is x, there is no x - 1 left, so x can't be used in the middle or at the end of another group. Therefore x must start the group [x, x+1, …, x+groupSize-1]; there is no other option.

Complexity analysis

Sorting the cards takes O(n log n). After that, each card either starts a new group or is consumed in an existing group at most once; since dictionary operations are O(1), the second phase finishes in O(n) time.
