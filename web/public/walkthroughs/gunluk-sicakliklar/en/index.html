Daily Temperatures

We keep the indices still waiting for a warmer day on a monotonically decreasing stack (waiting). When a new day is warmer than the day on top of the stack, we compute the answers of the waiting days one by one and pop them off the stack.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    Keep the waiting days on a stack

    We push the indices of the days that haven't seen a warmer day yet onto the waiting stack.

  2. 2

    Resolve them when a warm day arrives

    As long as today's temperature is greater than that of the waiting.Peek() day, we remove that day with Pop() and write the difference day - earlier.

  3. 3

    Push today onto the stack too

    After resolving the colder days that were waiting, we push today's index with waiting.Push(day) so it can wait for its own warmer day.

Why is it so efficient?

O(n) time despite the nested loop

Even though there is a while inside the for, each day's index enters the stack exactly once (Push) and leaves it at most once (Pop). The total number of operations never exceeds 2n: O(n) time.

Why does it stay monotonically decreasing?

Because we pop the top element as soon as a day warmer than it arrives, the temperatures of the days left on the stack are always ordered from largest to smallest (or equal), from bottom to top.

Why don't equal temperatures resolve anything?

The problem asks for a warmer (>) day. So a new 70° that arrives while a 70° is waiting pops nothing; both wait together until 71° arrives.

What happens to the days left on the stack at the end?

In C#, a new int[n] array starts filled with 0. Days still waiting on the stack when the loop ends have no warmer day to their right; their answers are already 0.
