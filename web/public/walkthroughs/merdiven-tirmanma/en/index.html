Climbing Stairs

You can follow every step of the solution here. Pick an n, press Next or click Play, and see how the number of ways to reach each step is computed from the two steps below it.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    Look at the last move

    Right before reaching a step, you were either on the step just below and took 1 step, or two steps below and took 2 steps. There is no other way.

  2. 2

    Add the two numbers below

    So the number of ways to reach a step is the sum of the ways to reach the step one below and the step two below: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13…

  3. 3

    Keep only the last two

    Climb from the bottom up with a single loop. Each iteration only needs the last two results, so two variables (twoBelow, oneBelow) are enough.

Why O(n) time and O(1) space?

Each step is computed once

We compute each step once: O(n) time. Whether the staircase has 5 steps or 45, we only keep three numbers (twoBelow, oneBelow, ways): O(1) space.

Why is the recursive method slow?

If we wrote the same formula as a recursive method (ClimbStairs(n - 1) + ClimbStairs(n - 2)), the same steps would be computed over and over. Even at n = 5, step 3 is computed 2 times and step 2 is computed 3 times; at n = 45 the method is called more than 2 billion times and LeetCode returns "Time Limit Exceeded".
