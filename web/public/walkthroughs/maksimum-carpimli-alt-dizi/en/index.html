Maximum Product Subarray

Follow step by step how a negative number can instantly turn the smallest negative product into the largest positive product, and why tracking both curMax and curMin at each step is therefore enough.

The idea in three steps

  1. 1

    Keep both the largest and the smallest

    Since negative × negative = positive, the smallest (negative) product so far can set a new record when multiplied by the next negative number.

  2. 2

    Swap roles on a negative number

    If the new number is n < 0, the larger product will become smaller and the smaller one larger; swapping curMax and curMin handles this switch.

  3. 3

    Extend or start over

    After a zero, or when the number alone is better, Math.Max(n, curMax * n) starts a new subarray, and the best result is kept in result.

Why is it so efficient?

How is it different from Kadane's algorithm?

In the maximum-sum subarray (Kadane), the smallest sum from the past has no chance of becoming the largest sum later. With products, because of sign changes, the most negative value is potentially the most valuable candidate.

Zeros don't need a special if

When n = 0 arrives, curMax and curMin automatically become 0. In the next step, the expression Math.Max(n, 0 * n) makes a fresh start directly from n.

Why is the swap correct?

curMax >= curMin always holds. When multiplied by a negative n, the inequality flips: curMin * n >= curMax * n. Swapping curMax and curMin before multiplying keeps the code simple.

O(n) time, O(1) space

Trying every subarray would take O(n²). Since each step needs only the previous position's curMax and curMin, the result is reached in a single loop with constant memory.
