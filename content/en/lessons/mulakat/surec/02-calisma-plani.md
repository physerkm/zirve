---
title: 12-Week Study Plan
summary: A realistic preparation schedule you can follow while working or studying. How much time to spend on what, how to measure progress, and the most common study mistakes.
---

The biggest problem in interview preparation is not a lack of knowledge but a **lack of a plan**: solving problems at random, avoiding weak topics, and practicing in conditions far removed from a real interview. This plan is designed for someone who can set aside 10–12 hours a week. If you have more time, shorten it; if your foundation is weak, extend the first phase.

## How to split your time

| Area | New grad / mid-level | Senior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Algorithms and data structures | 65% | 40% |
| System design | 10% | 35% |
| Language and domain knowledge (C#, JS, React…) | 15% | 10% |
| Behavioral preparation | 10% | 15% |

As seniority grows, the weight shifts from coding to design; but coding interviews never disappear at any level.

## Phase 1 — Foundation (weeks 1–4)

Goal: be able to **recognize** the core data structures and the most common patterns.

| Week | Topic | On this site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Complexity analysis, arrays, hash tables | [Algorithms › Fundamentals](/tracks/algoritmalar) |
| 2 | Two pointers, sliding window, binary search | [Algorithms › Patterns](/tracks/algoritmalar) |
| 3 | Linked list, stack, queue | [Algorithms › Data Structures](/tracks/algoritmalar) |
| 4 | Trees, DFS, BFS | [Algorithms › Data Structures](/tracks/algoritmalar) |

Every week: read the topic, solve the related **easy** problems, and take the [topic quiz](/quizzes) at the end of the week. Speed doesn't matter in this phase; what matters is being able to explain why you solved each problem the way you did.

## Phase 2 — Going deeper (weeks 5–8)

Goal: be able to solve **medium** problems in 25–30 minutes.

| Week | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Graphs, heaps, topological sort |
| 6 | Dynamic programming (one-dimensional) |
| 7 | Backtracking, intervals, greedy methods |
| 8 | Mixed review; introduction to system design |

Start **timing yourself** in this phase. If you can't solve a problem in 30 minutes, look at the hints first; if you're still stuck, read the solution, close it, and rewrite it **from scratch** the next day. Reading a solution and saying "I get it" is not learning.

If you're preparing for senior roles, spend 3–4 hours a week on system design starting in week 5: read the lessons in the [System Design](/tracks/sistem-tasarimi) track, and each week design one classic question on paper while explaining it out loud.

## Phase 3 — Interview conditions (weeks 9–12)

Goal: be able to show what you know **under pressure, while talking**.

- **Mock interviews.** At least twice a week, with a friend or on a mock interview platform. Solving alone and solving while explaining to someone are different skills, and the interview measures the second one.
- **Mixed problems.** Solve problems without knowing their topic. In a real interview, nobody says "this is a sliding window question."
- **Behavioral stories.** Write 6–8 of your stories [in STAR format](/tracks/mulakat/star-yontemi) and rehearse them out loud.
- **Company-specific preparation.** In the last two weeks, adjust your focus to the target company's [process and areas of emphasis](/tracks/sirketler).
- **Language review.** Know your interview language's collections, string operations, and sorting API by heart; don't lose time looking up syntax.

Don't try to learn new topics in the final week. Review the problems you've solved and your mistake log, and get enough sleep.

## Measuring progress

"How many problems have I solved" is a misleading measure. Track these instead:

- **Unaided solve rate:** of your last 10 medium problems, how many did you solve on time without a hint?
- **Pattern recognition time:** after reading a problem, how long does it take you to find the approach?
- **Mistake log:** one line for every problem you got stuck on: what did you miss? If the same kind of mistake keeps recurring, that's the topic you need to study.

**Truly** understanding and reviewing 80–100 problems is more effective than skimming through 200.

## Common study mistakes

- **Studying only the topics you're comfortable with.** Progress happens where you struggle.
- **Looking at the solution too early.** Struggle for at least 20–30 minutes. The learning is in that struggle.
- **Never looking at the solution.** Spending two hours on a single problem is also inefficient.
- **Studying silently.** You'll need to talk in the interview; think out loud even when you're solving alone.
- **Leaning on your editor.** Make sure you can also write code in a plain editor without autocomplete.
- **Skipping review.** If you can't solve a problem again a week later, you haven't learned it. Use spaced repetition: 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.
- **Burning out.** Rest completely one day a week. This is a marathon.

## Summary

- Three phases: foundation (recognize patterns) → going deeper (solve on time) → interview conditions (solve while talking).
- Split your time according to your level; as seniority grows, design and behavioral preparation gain weight.
- Track your unaided solve rate and your recurring mistakes, not the problem count.
- Mock interviews are not optional.
